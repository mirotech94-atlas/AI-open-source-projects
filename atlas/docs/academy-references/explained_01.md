## How they open

IBM Technology's longer video opens by naming the confusion between the terms and saying it will answer the questions viewers left on an earlier video. Its shorter video opens by writing three candidate equations (AI versus ML, AI equals ML, something else) and asking which is right. Simplilearn opens on an animated character, a graphic designer who hears about a tool that makes images, and follows her curiosity. None shows the finished picture of all four terms first.

## The order they explain things

- IBM (long): AI as matching or exceeding human abilities, a timeline from early research and expert systems, machine learning as learning from data rather than being programmed, deep learning as layered neural networks inside it, then generative AI and foundation models inside that, and adoption.
- IBM (short): a working definition of AI (discover, infer, reason), machine learning as prediction from data with supervised and unsupervised kinds, deep learning as a subset that does not always show its work, then the other parts of AI that are not machine learning.
- Simplilearn: what generative AI is, uses (text, images, music, healthcare), how an image model turns a prompt into a picture, tokens, feedback, then a closing quiz.

## Examples and analogies

- Nested circles on a lightboard, each term drawn inside the last (IBM).
- A guessing game: predict the next shape in a sequence, then a surprise breaks the pattern (IBM), to show learning from examples.
- Older AI work in rule-based languages and expert systems, as the part of AI that is not machine learning (IBM).
- A prompt for a cat wearing sunglasses, used to explain how an image model links words to pictures (Simplilearn).

## Beginner mistakes they address

- Treating AI and machine learning as the same word (IBM).
- Thinking all AI learns from data; rule-based systems are AI too (IBM).
- Expecting a deep learning model to explain its answer (IBM).
- Thinking generative AI only means chatbots; it also makes images, audio and video (Simplilearn, IBM).

## Pacing

Measured: 5 to 10 minutes. The IBM videos are one presenter at a lightboard drawing as he talks, calm and unhurried; Simplilearn is fast narration over animation. IBM's long video and Simplilearn name chapters by the term being explained, the searchable pattern; IBM's short one has none.

## What they do badly, and what we do better

- The final picture arrives at the end; we open on the four nested boxes complete, then build them.
- Few everyday tools are placed in the boxes; we put a tool the viewer already uses in each one.
- Simplilearn states model training details as general fact without sources; we say only what we can point to.
- No exercise; ours ends with the viewer placing three of their own tools.

## What we take (structure only)

1. One card with the nested boxes from the first second.
2. One box at a time, widest first, each with one familiar example.
3. Name the part of AI that is not machine learning, so the boxes are not read as the whole field.
4. A short quiz placing a tool in a box.
5. The viewer's own three tools as the checkpoint. This fits the AI Explained rule: a concept anchored on something the viewer has seen, taught as a diagram where no live demo is shown.
