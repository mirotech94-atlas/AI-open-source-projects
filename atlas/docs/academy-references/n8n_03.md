## How they open

The search for managing running workflows found no tutorial dedicated to it; the three kept touch it from the side, and the note says so. The Workflow ROI video opens by telling beginners to automate one process end to end before anything clever, then lists the pattern it will build. The Six Flow (AI Hustlers) video opens with a channel greeting and the promise of instant alerts for any failing workflow, then a like-and-subscribe ask. The DailyAi.Studio video opens as a spoken opinion piece on moving business logic out of code, with no result on screen.

## The order they explain things

- Workflow ROI: the node-chain mental model, a form trigger, an Edit Fields step that cleans the input, an IF rule, both branches, a record of the outcome, two test leads, then reading the execution history to debug, and a checklist.
- Six Flow: a sheet with fixed columns, the Error Trigger with its sample data, a sheet row and a Slack message mapped from it, a deliberately broken scheduled workflow, linking it to the error workflow in its settings, then the live failure.
- DailyAi.Studio: why a no-code tool for business rules, why not an AI coding tool, the execution history as the main benefit for a team, scale, patience, then a long walk through one real customer workflow (PDF in, parse, store, database).

## Examples and analogies

- A lead-intake form routed by a plain qualification rule (Workflow ROI).
- A scheduled workflow broken on purpose with code that cannot run, to trigger the alert (Six Flow).
- Lab results arriving as PDFs from different vendors, parsed and stored for a clinic customer (DailyAi.Studio).
- The execution history described as the place a support team looks first, and from which a run can be repeated (DailyAi.Studio).

## Beginner mistakes they address

- Starting with many apps or an AI agent instead of one process you understand (Workflow ROI).
- Letting each later step cope with messy input instead of cleaning it once (Workflow ROI).
- Reaching for AI where a fixed rule is clearer (Workflow ROI).
- Expecting an error workflow to fire without linking it in the failing workflow's settings (Six Flow).
- Getting frustrated when a single node re-run behaves oddly; re-run the whole test instead (DailyAi.Studio).

## What they do badly, and what we do better

- None shows the Executions list, a retry from it, workflow history or tags as its subject; managing a live workflow is a side note. Our lesson makes it the subject and shows each screen.
- Six Flow asks the viewer to copy a sheet layout by pausing; we give a free lab file.
- Opinion and product pitch mixed into a tutorial (DailyAi.Studio); we keep opinions in their own labelled series.
- Step-number chapter names; ours name the screen or action.

## What we take (structure only)

1. Start from one running workflow the viewer already has (the lab file), not a new build.
2. Show where its runs are listed, open a failed one, and read it.
3. Retry it from the list, then show what changed.
4. Then the housekeeping: publish, history, tags, in that order of need.
5. End on a checklist the viewer can run weekly.
